### Chapter 1: System Variables and Baseline Respiration Constants

Closed-loop life support architectures deployed in microgravity orbital environments depend upon strict mathematical balancing of metabolic consumption inputs and biological gas regeneration outputs. This document establishes the foundational parameters for the ORBITAL_AGRI_002 volume, specifically addressing system variables, crew metabolic loads, and baseline plant productivity constraints within aerospace bio-engineering applications. 

To maintain baseline human physiological requirements, the habitat life support system tracks distinct mathematical constants. The daily oxygen requirement per crew member, designated as $O_2\text{req}$, is established at $0.84$ kg per individual per twenty-four-hour period. This consumption rate incorporates basal metabolic oxidation, minor operational leakage, and metabolic workloads typical of orbital maintenance tasks. The total crew personnel count, designated as $N_{\text{crew}}$, is held constant at $6$ active personnel. Multiplying these two primary values yields the total daily oxygen demand for the orbital habitat. Specifically, six crew members consuming zero point eighty-four kilograms of oxygen each day generate a cumulative metabolic oxygen deficit of five point zero four kilograms per twenty-four-hour cycle that biological systems must regenerate.

Counterbalancing human metabolic consumption requires controlled agricultural production within specialized cultivation bays. The daily oxygen yield per square meter of crop canopy, denoted as $Y_{\text{plant}}$, is measured at zero point zero five kilograms per square meter per day. This yield coefficient applies to a standard dwarf crop variant optimized for microgravity phototrophic performance. The total active cultivation area, designated as $A_{\text{cultivation}}$, provides a maximum baseline footprint of one hundred twenty point zero square meters. Operating this maximum cultivation area at baseline yield parameters produces six point zero kilograms of diatomic oxygen per day. Comparing this maximum biological output against the baseline human consumption requirement reveals an operational delta that shifts depending on the crop growth phase.

Because $Y_{\text{plant}}$ is fixed at zero point zero five kilograms per square meter, gas balance equations remain strictly linear relative to canopy expansion.
